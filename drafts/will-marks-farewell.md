# Farewell to Will Marks + Mike J stepping in as interim DSM lead

*Draft announcement — intended for #general (Slack). Sources at the bottom.*

---

## Draft message

Team,

Some bittersweet news. After nearly four years, **Will Marks** is moving on from Mutinex.

Bitter, because Will has been part of the furniture since 2023 and it's genuinely hard to imagine the place without him. Sweet, because what he's built here will keep paying off long after he's gone — and because his move opens a real opportunity for someone inside this business to step up into an expanded role.

**It started in Marketing Science**, where Will showed the instinct he's carried ever since: *if something is hard, go build the thing that makes it easy.* The spend-reconciliation tooling that stopped MarSci chasing phantom QA errors. The refresh QA hauled out of Tableau and into Hex, so the whole team finally worked off one clear traffic-light view.

And the insight work. Will built the cross-customer analysis that turned our own platform into a source of category benchmarks — *media contributes between 5% and 8% of all sales in Auto*, that kind of thing — and it's been in Henry's hands for senior conversations ever since. When Hen needed platform-wide numbers on Nine the day before meeting their CEO, Will had the benchmarks and two customer insight packs in the thread inside twenty minutes, re-cut to order, with the sharing permissions already checked. That's the standard.

**Then he took on Data Solutions** as team lead, and picked up the metric that matters most to our customers: recency. And he moved it.

**#SpeakingNumerically**
- In **April, 21 customers were sitting above 100 days** of recency. By **July we'd smashed through the 70-day barrier** — off an all-time high of **87 model releases in 30 days**.
- Customers with data **less than three months old went from 73% to 86% in a single month**.
- A record **68% with data from the current or prior month** — the best we have ever run.
- And by August, **7 customers tracking to sub-90-day onboarding** — the bar we'd set at the start of the year.

Look at the denominator, though. Our customer base grew from **73 to 101** over the same window. Will's team absorbed a 40% jump in customers *while* driving recency down. That's not luck, and it isn't grind — that's capacity he deliberately built.

**And the last six months have been his best.** Will took some very direct feedback about a function under strain and turned it into a step change — in the numbers, and in how the team showed up. He owned escalations rather than routing them. He was honest about the customer experience when it wasn't good enough (like getting the HiPages model design right). And he consistently pushed credit and visibility onto his team rather than himself.

Will — thank you, mate. Personally, I've learnt so much from you over the last 18 months and enjoyed working with you immensely. You leave this space in a far better state than you found it, and you leave behind a team that is better because you led it. Go well. :clap:

---

**And now for the sweeter, exciting follow-on news.**

**Mike J** steps up as **interim leader of the Data Solutions space**, to continue building for at least the next two quarters.

We deliberately looked inward here. We have exceptional people in this business, and when a role like this opens up, the right first question is *who here is ready to rise to it?* This is a genuine opportunity for career progression, and I'd encourage everyone to see it that way — for Mike now, and for the people who'll grow into the space he leaves behind.

Most of you already know Mike through **Clive** — the AI harness he built, now sitting at the very centre of how the DSM team works. Shared skills, a persistent knowledge base for every customer, and live connections into DataOS, Dagster, BigQuery, Linear, Salesforce, Slack, Hex and Release Controller. In practice: a DSM can diagnose a connector discrepancy, trace spend across all four pipeline layers, and fix it themselves — work that used to be a ticket, an escalation, and a wait.

And the impact is real. With Graham's DataOS toolkit plugged into Clive, the team now self-resolves entire categories of issues that previously needed engineering, and Tier support requests are trending at their **lowest rolling average ever** — while our customer base grew past 100. It's rolling out to Marketing Science now, with Jess road-testing it for weeks. Her verdict: *"Clive is awesome."*

That instinct — take the thing one person does brilliantly and turn it into a system everybody can use — is exactly why I'm so excited about Mike in this seat. The opportunity ahead is to scale it across the whole team, in Australia and globally. If every DSM and every MarSci is operating with that leverage, capacity stops being a headcount conversation entirely.

Will, thank you. Mike, congratulations. We're all right behind you both.

Onwards and upwards :rocket:
Shane
