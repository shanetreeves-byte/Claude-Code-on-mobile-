# Farewell to Will Marks + Mike J stepping in as interim DSM lead

*Draft announcement — intended for #general (Slack). Verify the numbers against the latest dashboards before posting; sources are listed at the bottom.*

---

## Draft message

Team,

After nearly four years, **Will Marks** is moving on from Mutinex. I want to take a moment to properly thank him, because his fingerprints are on an enormous amount of what we do every day.

**Marketing Science.** Will started with us in Marketing Science, and even then his instinct was the same one he's carried through his whole time here: if something is hard, build the thing that makes it easy. He built the spend-reconciliation tooling that stopped MarSci chasing false-positive QA errors, drove the customer narrative around our open-source MMM evaluation framework — holding *every* vendor, us included, accountable to the quality of their models — and migrated the refresh QA out of Tableau into Hex so the whole team could work off one traffic-light view. Quiet, unglamorous, deeply leveraged work.

**Data Solutions.** Will then moved into the Data Solutions space and took on the team lead role, where he owned the metric that matters most to our customers: recency. The numbers tell the story better than I can.

- Average recency came down from **91 days to 75** across the back half of 2025, then broke the **70-day barrier** in July this year — off the back of an all-time high of **87 releases in 30 days**.
- **68% of customers** with data from the current or prior month, a record when he set it, with the CVI recency metric now tracking toward **81%**.
- **50 onboardings and rebuilds** delivered in 2025, and a clear push to sub-90-day onboarding.

What makes those numbers remarkable is the denominator. Our customer base grew from **73 to 101** over the same period, and the team absorbed that growth without the wheels coming off. That is capacity Will built — through Project Spectra to fix agency data supply at the source, through the automated slippage reporting that put refreshes back on track before anyone had to ask, and through relentlessly pushing the team to solve problems themselves rather than queue for someone else to.

**The last six months in particular.** I want to be specific here, because it was genuinely impressive. Will took some very direct feedback on a function that had slipped, and turned it into a step change — in the numbers, and in how the team showed up. As I said to him at the time, *"it definitely feels like a step change in how the team are leaning in, and also how you're showing up to bring everyone along."* He owned escalations rather than routing them, was honest about the customer experience where it wasn't good enough, and actively pushed credit and visibility onto his team rather than himself. Resilience, and a genuine willingness to learn in public, are hard things to find. Will has both.

Will — thank you. You leave the space in a materially better state than you found it, and you leave a team that is better because you led it. We'll miss you.

---

**Mike J** steps in as **interim leader of the Data Solutions space for the next six months.**

Most of you will already know Mike through **Clive** — the AI harness he built and which now sits at the centre of how the DSM team works. Clive centralises and streamlines the team's entire Claude usage: shared skills, a persistent knowledge base per customer, and live connections into DataOS, Dagster, BigQuery, Linear, Salesforce, Slack, Hex and Release Controller. In practice it means a DSM can diagnose a connector discrepancy, trace spend across all four pipeline layers, and fix the problem themselves — work that used to be a ticket and a wait.

The impact has been real and measurable. With Graham's DataOS toolkit plugged into Clive, the team now self-resolves whole categories of issues that previously needed engineering, and Tier support requests have been trending at their lowest rolling average ever *while* our customer base has grown to over 100. Clive is now rolling out to Marketing Science, with Jess road-testing it for weeks — her verdict was simply *"Clive is awesome."*

That instinct — take the thing one person does well, and turn it into a system everyone can use — is exactly why I'm excited about Mike in this seat. The opportunity in front of him is to take what he's built personally and scale it across the entire team, in Australia and globally. If we can get every DSM and every MarSci operating with the leverage Clive gives, the capacity question stops being a headcount question.

Mike, congratulations — we're all behind you.

Shane
