# Farewell to Will Marks + Mike J stepping in as interim DSM lead

*Draft announcement — intended for #general (Slack). Verify the numbers against the latest dashboards before posting; sources are listed at the bottom.*

---

## Draft message

Team,

Some bittersweet news to share. After nearly four years with us, **Will Marks** is moving on from Mutinex.

Bitter, because Will has been part of the furniture since 2023 and it's genuinely hard to imagine the place without him. Sweet, because what he's built here is going to keep paying off long after he's gone — and because his move creates a real opportunity for people inside this business to step up into an expanded role. More on that below.

First, though, a proper thank you. Will's fingerprints are on an enormous amount of what we do every day.

**It started in Marketing Science.** Will joined us there, and even then he showed the instinct he's carried through his entire time at Mutinex: *if something is hard, go build the thing that makes it easy.* He built the spend-reconciliation tooling that stopped MarSci chasing phantom QA errors. He drove the customer narrative around our open-source MMM evaluation framework — a genuinely bold move that held every vendor in this industry, ourselves included, accountable to the quality of their models. And he migrated the refresh QA out of Tableau and into Hex so the whole team could finally work off one clear traffic-light view. Quiet, unglamorous, enormously leveraged work. Classic Will.

**Then he took on Data Solutions.** Will moved into the Data Solutions space as team lead and picked up the metric that matters most to our customers: recency. And he moved it.

- Average recency came down from **91 days to 75** across the back half of 2025 — then smashed through the **70-day barrier** in July this year, off the back of an all-time high of **87 releases in 30 days**.
- A record **68% of customers** with data from the current or prior month, with the CVI recency metric now tracking toward **81%**.
- **50 onboardings and rebuilds** delivered in 2025, and a hard push toward sub-90-day onboarding.

Here's what makes those numbers genuinely special, though: look at the denominator. Our customer base grew from **73 to 101** over the same window. Will's team absorbed a 40% jump in customers *while* driving recency down. That's not luck, and it isn't grind — that's capacity he deliberately built. Through Project Spectra, going after agency data supply at the source. Through automated slippage reporting that put refreshes back on track before anyone had to ask. Through relentlessly pushing his team to solve problems themselves rather than join a queue.

**And the last six months have been his best.** I want to be specific here, because it was a genuinely impressive thing to watch. Will took some very direct feedback about a function under strain and turned it into a step change — in the numbers, and in how the team showed up. As I said to him at the time: *"it definitely feels like a step change in how the team are leaning in, and also how you're showing up to bring everyone along."* He owned escalations rather than routing them. He was honest about the customer experience when it wasn't good enough. And he consistently pushed credit and visibility onto his team rather than himself.

Resilience, and the willingness to learn in public, are rare things. Will has both in spades.

Will — thank you, mate. You leave this space in a far better state than you found it, and you leave behind a team that is better because you led it. Go well. 👏

---

**Now for the exciting part.**

**Mike J** steps up as **interim leader of the Data Solutions space for the next six months.**

We deliberately looked inward here. We have exceptional people in this business, and when a role like this opens up, the right first question is *who here is ready to rise to it?* This is a genuine opportunity for career progression, and I'd encourage everyone to see it that way — for Mike now, and for the people who'll grow into the space he leaves behind.

Most of you already know Mike through **Clive** — the AI harness he built, which now sits at the very centre of how the DSM team works. Clive centralises and streamlines the team's entire Claude usage: shared skills, a persistent knowledge base for every customer, and live connections into DataOS, Dagster, BigQuery, Linear, Salesforce, Slack, Hex and Release Controller. In practice, it means a DSM can diagnose a connector discrepancy, trace spend across all four pipeline layers, and fix it themselves — work that used to be a ticket, an escalation, and a wait.

And the impact is real. With Graham's DataOS toolkit plugged into Clive, the team now self-resolves entire categories of issues that previously needed engineering. Tier support requests have been trending at their **lowest rolling average ever** — while our customer base grew past 100. Clive is now rolling out to Marketing Science, with Jess road-testing it for weeks. Her verdict: *"Clive is awesome."*

That instinct — take the thing one person does brilliantly, and turn it into a system everybody can use — is exactly why I'm so excited about Mike in this seat. The opportunity ahead of him is to take what he's built personally and scale it across the whole team, in Australia and globally. If every DSM and every MarSci is operating with the leverage Clive gives, capacity stops being a headcount conversation entirely.

Mike, congratulations. We're all right behind you. 🚀

Shane
